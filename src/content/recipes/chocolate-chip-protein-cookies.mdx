## Why this recipe works

The chocolate gets plenty of room to shine, with a small amount of instant coffee in the dough for a deeper flavor. Applesauce keeps the mixture moist, and powdered peanut butter adds another layer without turning this into a full peanut-butter cookie.

The important bit is the pause before baking. Chill the shaped dough for 30–60 minutes, then give the cookies time to cool and set afterward. Warm chocolate is tempting, but a hot cookie is much more fragile.

## Choose the right protein powder

This recipe calls for a vanilla whey-and-casein blend. Protein powders absorb liquid differently, so switching to whey-only, plant-based or beef protein can change the dough substantially. A generic “one scoop” won’t be reliable either: weigh out 2.8 oz / 80 g.

PB2 is the powdered peanut butter in the recipe. Another powdered peanut butter is a possible alternative, but choose a similar product and expect small changes in sweetness and dough texture. Keep the light butter, granulated sweetener and brown-sugar replacement separate from the flour mixture until you cream them together; the sweetener only goes in once.

## Tools worth having

A digital scale keeps your protein baking consistent and helps you divide the batch evenly.

The <AmazonLink product="oxoMediumCookieScoop">OXO medium cookie scoop</AmazonLink> is an optional convenience for sticky dough. Two spoons work too. Divide the complete batch into twelve equal portions; one scoopful is not automatically one cookie.

A sturdy baking sheet and oven-safe parchment make lifting the cooled cookies easier. You don’t need a mixer with lots of attachments: a whisk, two mixing bowls and a flexible spatula cover the job.
