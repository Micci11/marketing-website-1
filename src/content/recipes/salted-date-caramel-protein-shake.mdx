## Why this recipe works

This is a generous shake, so grab a big glass. The homemade date caramel is a separate little batch: you use just 1.4 oz / 40 g in the shake and keep the rest for another round. Once that’s ready, the shake itself takes about five minutes.

## Getting the texture right

A smooth vanilla protein powder is a good fit here. Choose a flavor you already enjoy mixed with milk, because its sweetness will come through in the finished shake.

The recipe uses 14.1–15.9 oz / 400–450 g ice. Start with the lower amount and blend thoroughly before adding the rest. More ice makes a larger, frostier shake, but it also needs enough blender power and room to move. Don’t pack a small cup beyond its maximum fill line.

For the caramel, weigh the dates if you can. Twelve dates can vary enormously in size; the target is about 8.5 oz / 240 g after pitting. A quick hot-water soak helps them blend more smoothly.

## Tools worth having

An ice-capable blender is the useful purchase here, not a fancy serving glass. Look for a jug with enough room for a bulky frozen drink and clear maximum-fill markings. A small spatula helps get the thick caramel out without wasting it.

The <AmazonLink product="ninjaProfessionalBL610">Ninja Professional BL610</AmazonLink> is an optional ice-crushing model with a full-size pitcher. Use an ice-capable blender you already own first, and split the mixture if needed. This model is for cold blending, not hot soup.

A kitchen scale also makes the caramel portion easy: measure 1.4 oz / 40 g straight into the blender rather than trying to estimate a sticky spoonful. Use an airtight container for the extra caramel.
