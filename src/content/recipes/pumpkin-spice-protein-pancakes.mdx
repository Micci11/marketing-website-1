## Why this recipe works

This makes one generous serving of three or four pancakes. Pumpkin brings the soft texture and warm color, while ginger and nutmeg round out the cinnamon. The whipped cream softens over the warm stack, and a little syrup plus one last sprinkle of cinnamon finishes it off.

The key is gentle cooking. Keep the pan over medium-low heat so the center has time to set before the outside gets too dark. Smaller pancakes are easier to flip, especially with a thick pumpkin batter.

## A few ingredient notes

Use plain pumpkin purée, not sweetened pumpkin pie filling. You’ll blend 1.4 oz / 40 g rolled oats into flour first, then add the 3.5 oz / 100 g pumpkin and the remaining batter ingredients.

Vanilla protein powders vary in sweetness and absorbency. Weigh out 1.1 oz / 30 g rather than relying on a scoop size, and start lightly with the monkfruit sweetener. The 3–5-minute rest gives the oat mixture time to thicken, so don’t rush straight from blending to pouring.

## Tools worth having

A nonstick skillet or griddle with steady heat makes this much easier. You want enough room to slide a thin spatula under each pancake without nudging the one next to it. If your pan is small, cook in batches rather than making one oversized pancake.

If you need a turner, the [OXO silicone flexible pancake turner](https://www.oxo.com/shop/kitchenware/utensils/spoons-scoopers/silicone-flexible-pancake-turner.html) has a broad, flexible head to support the flip. Check that it fits comfortably in your skillet; a smaller nonstick-safe turner may be easier in a little pan.

A compact blender that can grind oats and a digital scale are the other useful basics. There’s no need for a special pancake machine; a reliable pan and a little patience do the work.
